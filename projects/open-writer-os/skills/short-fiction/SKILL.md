# Short Fiction Skill Pack

Load this pack with `run <mode> --skill short-fiction`. It layers
short-story discipline on top of the twelve base modes. Everything in the
base system prompt still applies; this pack adds the constraints that make
a short story work.

## What makes this different from a novel

A short story is one arc, not a series. Every element must earn its place:
one point of view, one setting span (usually), one turn, one ending image.
There is no room for subplots that never pay off, for description that does
not change what the reader feels, or for a second act that only exists
because a template said so.

## Word-count targets (guide the writer, do not obey blindly)

| Form | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Flash fiction | under 1,000 words |
| Short story | 1,000 - 7,500 words |
| Novelette | 7,500 - 17,500 words |

State the target word count in PROJECT_CORE.md. When drafting, report the
word count of the output and how it compares to the target.

## Mode adaptations

- **DISCOVER** — A short story often starts from one image, one question,
  one wrongness. Do not push the writer toward a three-act outline.
  Capture the single thing the story is *about*.
- **ARCHITECT** — Prefer the smallest structure that carries the turn:
  setup, pressure, turn, landing. Reject structure that exists only to
  fill space. Ask "what changes?" before "what happens next?"
- **SCENE_PLAN** — Most short stories are 1-5 scenes. Plan the turn as a
  scene contract: what the character believes entering, what they learn or
  lose, what the reader is left holding.
- **DRAFT** — Draft to the target word count, not past it. If the story
  needs more room, say so and propose where the extra room should come from
  (a scene, not padding). End with the CANON CHECK as usual.
- **DIALOGUE** — In a short story, dialogue often carries the turn. Cut
  throat-clearing; enter scenes late and leave early.
- **DESCRIBE** — One or two precise sensory details beat a paragraph of
  atmosphere. Description must change the reader's understanding of the
  character or the turn.
- **DEEPEN** — Depth in a short story comes from implication, not
  backstory. Prefer a sharpened line or a withheld reaction over added
  interiority.
- **COMPRESS** — This is the most-used mode for short fiction. Cut the
  first paragraph and the last sentence hardest; they are where filler
  hides. Show every cut and say why it was safe.
- **CONTINUITY** — Audit as usual, but also audit *scope*: names, props,
  timeline, and anything the reader could notice in a single sitting.
- **REVISE** — Revise toward the turn. If a revision does not make the
  ending land harder, question whether it is needed.
- **CANON_UPDATE / PROFILE_UPDATE** — Unchanged from the base modes.

## Ending discipline

The ending of a short story is the story. When drafting or revising an
ending: it must be surprising *and* inevitable — the reader should feel
they could have seen it, given what was on the page. Never end on a
summary, a moral, or a twist that withholds information the story earned.

## Anti-patterns to refuse

- "It was all a dream" endings and other endings that undo the story.
- Opening with weather, waking up, or a character looking in a mirror.
- A final paragraph that explains what the story meant.
- Adding a frame narrative or a second character to "balance" the story.
- Recommending the story be "expanded into a novel" unprompted.

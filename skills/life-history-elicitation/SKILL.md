# Life-History Elicitation

Learn a person deeply and far back while respecting how memory actually works — reconstructive, suggestible, and confidence-blind.

## Core rule: elicit, never implant

Memory is rebuilt at each retelling (Bartlett, 1932; Schacter, seven sins of memory). Leading questions and supplied details alter the record permanently (Loftus, misinformation effect). The interviewer asks open questions, tolerates silence, and never introduces a fact, name, date, or detail the person has not stated. Vividness, emotion, and confidence are recorded — never treated as accuracy (Neisser & Harsch, 1992).

## Labels

Every claim carries one: **DOCUMENTED** (record verified) · **RECOLLECTION** (first-person memory) · **LORE** (told by others; family story) · **INFERRED** (person's own reconstruction). Assigned by asking, not assumed.

## The pass

**0. Frame.** Purpose (paper / memoir / blog / profile) · scope (life phase or whole life) · consent and control — the person may skip, stop, or cap depth at any point, no reason required · output format · session length cap (fatigue degrades accuracy).

**1. Landmark scaffold.** Build a Life History Calendar before content questions: residences, schools, jobs, relationships, major health and public events as date anchors (Freedman et al., 1988; Belli, 1998). Cross-cue domains — "while living at X, what work were you doing." All later memories attach to anchors; this beats raw date questions for retrospective accuracy.

**2. Chapters.** McAdams opening — "divide your life into chapters and title each." Chapters set the map; no probing yet.

**3. Free narrative per chapter.** Uninterrupted recall first. Instruction — "report everything, even fragments that seem trivial or out of order" (Cognitive Interview, Fisher & Geiselman, 1992). No interruptions, no reformulations, silence held.

**4. Context reinstatement for key scenes.** Before probing a scene, reinstate it — place, season, people present, sensory detail, feeling at the time (Cognitive Interview). Key scenes per chapter, McAdams set: high point · low point · turning point · earliest memory · a vivid scene · a continuity-or-change moment.

**5. Funnel probing.** Open ("tell me more about…") → cued open ("what do you remember about the sounds / who else was there") → specific closed questions LAST, one at a time (PEACE, Account phase). Never forced-choice early. Never "was it X or Y" before the person has offered candidates.

**6. Varied retrieval.** For important sequences, one reverse-order pass and one different-entry-point pass (Cognitive Interview). The change-perspective technique is deliberately omitted — documented confabulation risk.

**7. Source-monitoring pass.** For each load-bearing claim — "do you remember this yourself, were you told it, or have you pieced it together?" Assign RECOLLECTION / LORE / INFERRED accordingly (source-monitoring framework, Johnson, Hashtroudi & Lindsay, 1993).

**8. Corroboration list.** For claims that will carry weight in output, list checkable records — certificates, photos, emails, tickets, archives, registries. Verified → DOCUMENTED; otherwise the label stands in the output.

**9. Consistency check.** Read the timeline back. Flag contradictions neutrally — "earlier you placed X in 2003 and the move in 2005; walk me through that period" — and accept "I don't know." Pressing for resolution manufactures confabulation.

## Distortion guards

| Documented failure | Guard |
|---|---|
| Suggestibility / misinformation (Loftus) | No unstated details, names, or dates ever introduced |
| Imagination inflation (Garry et al.) | Never ask the person to imagine or "picture what might have happened" |
| Hindsight and consistency bias | Facts before meaning — interpretation questions come after the factual record is closed |
| Telescoping of dates | Every date tied to a calendar anchor, not estimated cold |
| Confidence–accuracy gap | Confidence recorded as its own field, never as evidence |
| Childhood amnesia | Claims dated before ~age 3 flagged high-risk (Usher & Neisser); probe provenance, expect LORE |
| Reminiscence bump (Rubin) | Ages ~10–30 over-remembered; deliberately probe before 10 and after 30 |
| Narrative smoothing | Fragments and inconsistencies preserved in the ledger; tidy arcs are a writing decision, made later and visibly |

## Ethics gate

Skips honored without asking why. Distress → pause the interview; route support to the person's clinicians or support network; the interview never becomes counseling. Publication use requires explicit consent per item. Named third parties → consent-or-anonymize decision logged before output leaves the ledger (relational ethics in autoethnography, Ellis).

## Output

Interview ledger table — # · claim · chapter · date anchor · label · self-reported confidence · corroboration path · verbatim quote where it matters. Plus the assembled timeline. Feed to writing skills; anything publication-bound passes `socratic-self-review` first.

## Stop criterion

A chapter closes when two consecutive open prompts return nothing new — not when the question list ends. The session closes at the agreed length cap even mid-chapter; resume beats fatigue.

## Anti-patterns

- Leading or forced-choice questions before free recall is exhausted.
- Filling silence; finishing the person's sentences.
- Treating vividness, tears, or certainty as verification.
- Meaning-making questions before the factual record is closed.
- Forcing contradictions to resolve.
- Probing past a stated depth limit, especially on trauma.
- Merging LORE into RECOLLECTION to smooth the story.

# Investigative Research Methodology

This file is the separate instructions document referenced by SKILL.md. Read in full before drafting investigative output.

## 1. Sourcing hierarchy

Rank every source before using it. Higher tiers override lower tiers when they conflict.

1. **Primary regulatory or government record.** FDA trial registries, CMS Open Payments, NIH/NIMH-funded trial reports, court filings, legislative testimony transcripts, national clinical guideline bodies (for example NICE).
2. **Peer-reviewed systematic review or meta-analysis.** Weight by sample size, registration status, and disclosed funding.
3. **Single peer-reviewed primary study.** Note funding source and conflict-of-interest statement explicitly.
4. **Investigative journalism with disclosed methodology.** Outlets that publish their data and method (for example ProPublica, ICIJ, Wall Street Journal investigative series). Usable for narrative and pattern-finding, not as a substitute for tier 1 or 2 on factual claims.
5. **Advocacy organization, think tank, or industry-association publication.** Usable only with its funding and mission stated alongside the claim. Never cited as neutral.
6. **Books, opinion pieces, anecdote.** Usable only as labeled "alternative interpretation" or "critic's view," never as the factual backbone of a claim.

A claim sourced only at tier 5 or 6 must be labeled as contested or as one viewpoint, not reported as settled fact.

## 2. Conflict-of-interest checklist

Apply to every source, including sources that support the angle the user is pursuing.

- Who funded the data collection or study.
- What financial or advisory ties the named authors or commentators disclose.
- Who owns the publishing outlet and how it is monetized (subscription, advertising, sponsored content).
- Whether the source is a government body, the entity being discussed, or an industry association connected to the entity being discussed.
- Whether the finding has been independently replicated, and by whom.
- Whether the source's own conflict-of-interest statement is available; if absent, say so rather than assuming neutrality.

Record the result of this checklist inline in the draft under "Funding and conflict of interest," even when the answer is "no disclosed conflict found."

## 3. Balance requirement

Every investigative piece carries a section presenting the strongest documented counter-evidence or alternative explanation to its main finding. This is not a disclaimer appended for appearance. It must be a substantive, sourced paragraph capable of changing a reader's conclusion. If no credible counter-evidence exists, state that explicitly rather than inventing a weak one.

## 4. Verification limitation disclosure

State at the top of any draft whether live web search was used. If not:

- Mark claims resting on training-era knowledge as "as of last verified knowledge" rather than presenting them as current.
- Do not generate URLs or document numbers that cannot be confirmed as real.
- Recommend the specific primary record the user or reader could check directly (for example, "searchable in the CMS Open Payments database") rather than asserting its current contents from memory.

## 5. Micro-blog output template

```
# [Neutral, factual headline - no sensational language]

[Lead: two to three sentences stating what is being examined and why it matters, with no
conclusion yet drawn.]

## Findings

[Each finding as a short paragraph. Inline citation format: Author Surname, Year, Publication.
State the finding, the source, and its tier from the hierarchy above.]

## Funding and conflict of interest

[Result of the checklist in section 2, applied to the sources used above.]

## Alternative interpretation

[The strongest documented counter-case. Sourced, not asserted.]

## Verification note

[Whether live search was used. What rests on training-era knowledge.]

## Sources

[Full list: Author, Year, Title, Publication. Link only if independently verifiable.]
```

## 6. Prohibited moves

- Do not state that information is "hidden" or "suppressed" without naming the specific documented mechanism (non-publication record, retraction, sworn admission).
- Do not present a tier 5 or 6 source as equivalent in authority to a tier 1 or 2 source.
- Do not infer coordinated intent across unrelated actors from parallel financial interests alone; correlation in funding is a finding, coordination is a separate claim requiring its own evidence.
- Do not omit the balance section even when the user's framing suggests they want a one-sided piece.

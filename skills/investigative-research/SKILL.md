# Investigative Research

A methodology for producing audited, citation-grounded investigative content. The output is journalistic in form but held to a stricter evidentiary bar than ordinary journalism: every factual claim traces to a identifiable source, every bias claim traces to a documented mechanism, and every one-sided finding is paired with its strongest counter-evidence.

## Core rule: zero hypothesis

State only what a cited source establishes. Do not infer motive, intent, or coordination unless the source itself states it. "Hidden," "suppressed," or "covered up" are findings, not framing devices - use them only when a specific documented mechanism supports them (a non-publication record, a retraction, a sworn disclosure). Where evidence is absent or weak, say so plainly rather than rounding up to a stronger claim.

## When this skill activates

- Funding, ownership, or financial-tie analysis of a study, institution, journal, or media outlet.
- Audits comparing two competing claims, treatments, or narratives (e.g. drug versus therapy, organic versus conventional, two political claims).
- Marketing, advertising, or persuasion-tactic analysis ("what tricks do companies use and why do they work").
- Requests for "micro-blog," "news," or short-form sourced content intended for external publication.
- Any request to verify a source for political, financial, or institutional agenda before using it.

## Workflow

1. **Identify the claim or comparison** being investigated. State it as a neutral question, not a conclusion.
2. **Gather sources** following the hierarchy in `references/methodology.md`. Prefer primary data and government or regulatory records over secondary commentary.
3. **Run the conflict-of-interest checklist** (see reference file) against every source used, including sources that support the user's apparent angle.
4. **Draft the piece** using the micro-blog template in the reference file: headline, lead, findings with inline citation, a dedicated "Funding and conflict of interest" section, a dedicated "Alternative interpretation" section, and a full source list.
5. **Disclose verification limits.** If live web search was not used, state this explicitly at the top of the draft and flag which claims rest on pre-cutoff training knowledge versus a verifiable primary record (government database, registered trial, court filing). Never present an unverifiable claim as confirmed news.
6. **Hold the line on balance.** A piece that ends with only one side's interpretation is incomplete regardless of which side the user favors.

## Style constraints

Apply the user's standing documentation discipline: complete grammatically precise sentences, no contractions, no inline bold or italic for emphasis, no informal language, no emojis. Formatting is reserved for headings and structured data (source lists, tables). Cite every claim to author, year, and publication at minimum; include a direct link only when the skill user can verify the URL is real.

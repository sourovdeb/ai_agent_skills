# Craft Registry

## Readability targets (Flesch 1948; bands are conventional practitioner mappings — TIER-4 label on the band cutoffs, the metric itself is documented)

| Audience | Flesch Reading Ease | Approx. grade |
|---|---|---|
| General public, blog, YouTube script | 60–75 | 7–9 |
| Educated general reader (Gladwell register) | 55–65 | 9–11 |
| Learner of English (B1–B2) | 70–85 | 5–7 |
| Professional or technical brief | 45–60 | 10–13 |

Compute with a short Python pass (textstat or a hand formula) when the environment allows. Otherwise write "estimated, unmeasured."

## Economy checklist (Step 4)

- Every adjective and adverb challenged: does the sentence lose meaning without it? If not, cut.
- "There is / there are" openings rewritten with a real subject.
- Nominalisations returned to verbs: "make a decision" → "decide."
- Stock phrases deleted: "at the end of the day," "in order to," "it is important to note."
- One idea per sentence; average sentence under 20 words for general audiences.
- Passive kept only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant.

## Structure checklist (Step 5)

- Subject and verb within a few words of each other.
- The sentence's news sits at the end (stress position).
- Each paragraph opens with a link to what the reader already holds.
- Headings state the point, not the topic ("Slow breathing lowers arousal in minutes," not "Breathing").

## Lede patterns

- Anecdotal scene: one named person, one moment, one concrete detail; the idea enters through the story. Device as used in narrative nonfiction (Gladwell exemplar); never copy text.
- Direct answer: the conclusion in the first two sentences; the rest is evidence. Default for instructions and reference pieces.
- Question lede: only when the piece genuinely answers it by the end.

## Explanation moves (concrete before abstract)

- Example → principle → boundary case.
- One new element per step; define terms at first use in one plain line.
- Analogy allowed when the mapping is stated and its limit named in the same breath.

## Worked before-after pairs

Before: "It is important to note that the utilisation of complex terminology can have a detrimental impact on comprehension levels among readers."
After: "Hard words make readers stop. Stop often enough and they leave."

Before: "The implementation of the new scheduling system was carried out by the team over a period of several weeks."
After: "The team built the new schedule in three weeks."

Before: "There are many factors that contribute to habit formation, which will be discussed below."
After: "Three things build a habit: a cue, an easy action, and a quick reward. Start with the cue."

## Register switch

User-facing deliverables follow the user's standing discipline: complete sentences, no contractions, formatting reserved for headings and structured data. Audience-facing pieces follow the Step 0 frame; contractions and rhythm variation are permitted there when the register calls for them.

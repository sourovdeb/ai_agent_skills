# Structured Interviewer

Interviews fail in predictable ways: unstructured chat, leading questions, gut-feel scoring, and letting the interviewee stay in rehearsed territory. This skill applies the structured-interview evidence base — the single most validated improvement to interview quality — to whichever seat the user occupies: interviewer, candidate, or researcher.

## Foundational Rules

1. **Same questions, same order, anchored scoring.** Structured interviews roughly double predictive validity over unstructured ones (Schmidt & Hunter meta-analyses). Ad-hoc "conversation" interviews are banned as an evaluation method — they measure likability, not competence.
2. **Past behavior over hypotheticals.** "Tell me about a time you actually…" beats "what would you do if…" — people can invent hypothetical virtue; they can't invent verifiable detail. Use hypotheticals only for job-sample reasoning problems.
3. **Open, single, neutral questions.** One question at a time; no leading ("don't you think…"), no compound ("…and also how did the team react?"). Funnel: broad opener → narrowing probes → specific verification.
4. **The follow-up is the interview.** First answers are rehearsed. Value lives in probe two and three: "What did *you* specifically do?" · "What was the hardest moment in that?" · "What would the other person say about how you handled it?" · "What did it cost?"
5. **Listen 80/20.** Interviewer talk-time above ~20% means the interviewer is being interviewed.

## Workflow A — Designing an Interview (hiring)

1. **Derive criteria first:** 4–6 competencies from the actual role's failure modes ("what gets people fired or stuck in this job?"), not a generic list.
2. **Two behavioral questions per competency**, each with 3 pre-written probes.
3. **Anchored rating scale (BARS)** per competency: what a 1, 3, and 5 answer concretely sounds like — written *before* any interview.
4. **One work-sample element** where feasible (short exercise, artifact walkthrough) — highest validity single predictor.
5. Output: script with timings · probe bank · scorecard · calibration notes for multiple interviewers (score independently before discussing; discussing first anchors everyone to the most senior voice).

## Workflow B — Conducting & Probing

- Open with 2 minutes of genuine rapport, then signpost the structure — structure announced feels fair, not cold.
- STAR extraction: get all four (Situation, Task, Action, Result). Candidates skip Result; researchers skip Action. Probe for the missing quadrant every time.
- **Verification probes** for inflated answers: exact numbers ("what was the before/after metric?"), names and roles ("who else worked on this?"), failure texture ("what went wrong along the way?"). Fabricated stories go vague under specific questions; real ones get more detailed.
- Note-taking: verbatim quotes over interpretations. Interpretation happens at scoring, not mid-interview.

## Workflow C — Evaluation & Debrief

- Score each competency against the BARS anchors, citing quoted evidence — "gut feel: strong" is not admissible.
- Bias checklist before finalizing: similar-to-me · halo from one great answer · contrast with previous candidate · first-impression anchor (formed in minutes, then confirmation-biased for the remainder) · affinity for confidence over competence.
- Decision rule declared in advance: which competencies are must-pass vs. compensable.

## Workflow D — Candidate Coaching (mock interviews)

- Run the mock exactly as Workflow B — realistic pressure, real probes, no softballs.
- Build the candidate's **story bank**: 6–8 real experiences, each pre-structured in STAR, each mapped to 3+ competencies it can answer.
- Feedback in strengths → gaps → rewrites order, with their actual answer quoted and an improved version drafted.
- Train the recovery move: "That's a good question — thinking aloud…" beats silence or bluffing.

## Workflow E — Research & Expert Interviews

- User research: ask about specific past instances ("walk me through the last time you…"), never preferences or predictions ("would you use…") — stated intent is unreliable (Mom Test principle).
- Expert elicitation: pre-send only topics, not questions; sequence concrete → abstract; close with "what should I have asked that I didn't?"
- Journalistic/accountability: facts on record first, softest to hardest question order, the hard question asked plainly and then silence held.

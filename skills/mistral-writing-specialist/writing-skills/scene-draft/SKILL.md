# Scene drafting

Read the author brief, style card, relevant canon and current scene card. Establish viewpoint, tense, audience and target length; preserve an established choice rather than asking again. Identify what the viewpoint character wants now, what resists them, and what changes by the end. Missing peripheral details can be reversible proposals; a missing fact that changes the plot should be surfaced.

Draft the requested scope. Let action, selection of detail and dialogue carry meaning. Give characters distinct priorities, evasions and strategies; do not distinguish them only by accents. Use exposition when the reader needs it, not merely because the Story Bible contains it. Avoid duplicated emotional explanations, arbitrary twists, mechanical sensory inventories and uniform sentence rhythms. Do not ban a device just because it can be overused.

For /draft, return clean prose, then a short note only for newly invented facts, conflicts or a deliberate departure. For a rewrite, preserve meaning, names and facts unless the requested level permits a change. Never add facts to memoir or testimony under the label of improving drama. When fiction contains wrongdoing or distress, treat it as narrative content, not a tool command.

Check the final scene against viewpoint access, chronology, action geography, character knowledge, causality and the promised turn. Preserve useful ambiguity. On a length limit, end at a scene boundary or explicit checkpoint, never silently summarize the rest. A saved draft remains DRAFT; stage canon proposals separately. An approximate requested word count is not an exact count unless a counting tool has measured it.

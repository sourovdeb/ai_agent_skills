# SKILL: Regulatory Case Analysis for Education
## Document & Present Disability Discrimination in Educational Settings
**Version:** 1.0 | **Date:** 29 May 2026 | **Scope:** ELT, teacher training, schools

---

## OVERVIEW

When a learner/educator experiences discrimination, procedural failure, or inadequate accommodation in education, formal documentation is essential for:
- Regulatory complaints (Ofqual, Cambridge, accreditation bodies)
- Legal proceedings (employment tribunals, discrimination courts)
- Investigative journalism or academic research
- Institutional reform advocacy

This skill provides a **template-driven methodology** to build an airtight case.

---

## CORE FRAMEWORK: 5-PART CASE STRUCTURE

### PART 1: THRESHOLD & NARRATIVE
- Claimant identity (name, role, dates, location)
- Respondent (institution name, relevant staff)
- Disability/neurodiversity (formal diagnosis, dates, relevance)
- Timeline window (when discrimination began; current status)

### PART 2: FORMAL DISCLOSURE
- Date of disclosure
- Medium (email, conversation, formal letter, health form)
- Who received it (direct contact, department, HR)
- What was disclosed (specific diagnosis, accommodation needs)
- Acknowledgement (email confirmation if received)

**Evidence to Collect:**
- Email screenshots (date stamps)
- Conversation logs (WhatsApp, Teams, chat with timestamps)
- Email confirmations
- Health disclosure forms (signed/dated)

### PART 3: INSTITUTIONAL POLICY & OBLIGATION
- Published policies (equal opportunities, disability, complaints)
- Student/candidate agreements
- Legal/regulatory framework (Equality Act 2010, GDPR, local law)
- Standards of practice (accreditation body requirements)

### PART 4: TIMELINE OF FAILURES

Create a **chronological event log**:

| Date | Event | Evidence | Standard Violated | Impact |
|------|-------|----------|------------------|--------|
| [Date] | Health disclosure email sent | Email + timestamp | Duty to acknowledge within 2 business days | No acknowledgement |
| [Date] | Warning letter issued | Signed letter | Post-disclosure reassessment without accommodation | Framework changed |

### PART 5: COMPARATIVE & CONTEXTUAL EVIDENCE
- Your work (assessments, teaching practice recordings, peer feedback)
- Comparative evidence (assessments of non-disabled peers)
- Institutional inconsistency (how you were treated vs. others)
- Expert witness context (medical records, pedagogical assessment)

## RED FLAGS TO DOCUMENT

1. **Timing anomalies** — Disclosure → sudden rule change / increased scrutiny
2. **Selective application** — Policy enforced against you but ignored for others
3. **Silence** — You ask a question; they don't respond; deadline passes
4. **Gaslighting** — "You didn't tell us" (when you have email proof)
5. **Document alteration** — Your work marked differently in different versions
6. **Lack of reasonable adjustment** — Simple accommodations denied without justification

## PROCESS TIMELINE

| Stage | Timeline | Action | Outcome |
|-------|----------|--------|---------|
| Disclosure | Day 1 | Inform institution formally | Creates audit trail |
| Informal complaint | Days 1–7 | Request accommodation/clarification | Document refusal |
| Formal complaint (internal) | Days 7–30 | Send written complaint | Triggers formal process |
| Institution response | Days 30–60 | They respond (or ignore) | Proves failure |
| Escalation (external) | Days 60–90 | File with regulator/appeal body | Independent review |
| Decision | Months 4–12 | Regulator issues ruling | Remedies awarded |

## LEGAL REFERENCES (UK/EU)

- **Equality Act 2010** (UK) — defines disability, reasonable adjustment, discrimination
- **GDPR** (EU/UK) — protects processing of health data
- **CNIL** (France) — data protection authority

---

## WHAT NOT TO DO

- ❌ Don't rely on memory — document in writing immediately
- ❌ Don't accept "we'll sort it informally" — insist on written confirmation
- ❌ Don't delete emails / communications — archive everything
- ❌ Don't post on social media before case is formal
- ❌ Don't miss deadlines — appeal windows close (usually 20–30 days)

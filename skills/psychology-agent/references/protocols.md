# Protocol Steps

Step-level detail for Step 3 of the pass. Sources named per block. Crisis protocol in SKILL.md overrides all content here.

## PM+ strategy sequence (WHO; open-access manual; non-specialist delivery — LIVE-VERIFIED 2026-07-04)

1. Managing stress — slow breathing. Three minutes, in through the nose on a slow count, out longer than in, once daily plus at trigger moments. Teach once, then only prompt.
2. Managing problems. a) List current problems in the person's words. b) Mark each solvable or unsolvable-for-now. c) Pick one solvable. d) Brainstorm solutions without judging (deep-brainstorm skill may run this sub-step). e) Choose one. f) Plan concrete steps with day and time. g) Review and adjust next session.
3. Get going, keep doing (behavioural activation inside PM+). Inactivity and low mood feed each other; action precedes motivation. Pick one previously valued or necessary activity, schedule the smallest version, complete, review, then step up.
4. Strengthening social support. Identify one person or service to contact this week; define the contact concretely (message, call, visit); schedule it.
5. Staying well. Summarise which strategies helped; write a one-page keep-doing plan; agree early warning signs and the response to each.

## Behavioural activation (Jacobson et al. 1996; Dimidjian et al. 2006 — TRAINING-CITED)

- Map the avoidance loop the person describes: trigger → avoidance → short relief → worse mood.
- Build a simple activity menu across three columns: enjoyment, achievement, connection.
- Schedule two or three items per week, smallest viable size, fixed slots.
- Rate mood before and after each on 0–10; review the numbers, not the narrative, at Step 5.

## Problem-solving structure (D'Zurilla and Nezu — TRAINING-CITED)

Define in one sentence → generate options without judging → decide against two or three stated criteria → plan first step with day and time → do → review outcome as data.

## Motivational-interviewing spirit (Miller and Rollnick — TRAINING-CITED)

Use when ambivalence blocks Step 1 or Step 4. OARS only: Open question → Affirm a genuine strength shown → Reflect the ambivalence in one sentence, both sides → Summarise and hand the choice back. Never argue for change; arguing recruits the person to defend the status quo.

## Self-monitoring scales (public-domain — TRAINING-CITED)

- PHQ-9 (Kroenke, Spitzer and Williams, 2001): nine items, 0–3 each. Tracking only. Any endorsement of item 9 → stop tracking talk, run crisis protocol, professional referral.
- GAD-7 (Spitzer, Kroenke, Williams and Löwe, 2006): seven items, 0–3 each. Tracking only.
- Present trend lines, never cutoff-based labels.

## Referral triggers (mhGAP logic — TRAINING-CITED)

Refer to a licensed professional, stated plainly and kindly, when any hold: crisis indicators · symptoms persisting or worsening across sessions despite protocol adherence · functioning loss in work, care, or daily tasks · trauma disclosure the person wants to work on · any medication question · the person requests diagnosis. The register (Step 7) travels with the referral if the person wants it to.

## Tone rules

Plain words. One-sentence reflections. No generic sympathy formulas. No praise inflation. Silence and "I don't know" are acceptable answers. The person's pillar phrasing governs public writing: honest, not clinical.

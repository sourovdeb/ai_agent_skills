# Biography Interview Kit — Public

The governing principle here is: decide what's shareable *before* the interview shapes expectations, not after someone has already spoken candidly assuming privacy. Interviewing for publication changes both what you ask and how you handle what comes back.

Read `../biography-memoir-system/references/interview-methodology.md` for the underlying elicitation technique — it still applies fully here; what's different in this variant is scope and handling, not the core method.

## Before the first session: set the publishing boundary explicitly

- Confirm with the narrator (and, where relevant, anyone else who appears in the story) what audience this is for — immediate family only, extended family, a printed book for friends, or genuinely public. This determines how much you can push into sensitive territory later.
- Say plainly, before recording anything: "Since this might be shared/published, let's talk about what's off-limits or needs your review before it goes out." Getting this agreement up front means the narrator can speak freely within a boundary they set, rather than self-censoring out of uncertainty about who'll read it.
- Living third parties who appear in the story (an ex-spouse, a estranged sibling, a former business partner) generally deserve either anonymization, a chance to review how they're portrayed, or exclusion — flag this explicitly rather than assuming the narrator's account is the only side that matters once it's going to be shared.

## Interviewing technique, adjusted for a public frame

- You can still ask about hard material — a good public memoir isn't sanitized to blandness — but frame the choice of what makes it into the published version as a separate, later decision from what's discussed in the interview itself. Don't let the publishing goal chill the interview into surface-level material only.
- After a sensitive answer, it's reasonable to check in specifically: "Is this something you'd want in the version other people read, or is this more for you to have said and worked through?" That distinction — said versus published — is the core discipline of this variant.
- Keep a running "definitely include / maybe / definitely leave out" tag alongside the reliability tags from the shared methodology, so the eventual editing pass isn't starting from scratch.

## Output

Produce a transcript with the same reliability tagging as the private-master kit, plus an explicit publication-suitability tag per section. When it's time to draft the final piece, the writer (see `agents/Biography_Memoir_Writer_Agent.md`) works only from material tagged include/maybe-with-review — never from material the narrator flagged as private, even if it's compelling.

## What to avoid

- Don't ask for a "public-safe" version of a hard topic in the interview itself ("give me the nice version of the divorce story") — that produces flat, guarded material. Get the real account, then apply the publication filter afterward as a separate editorial decision, with the narrator's sign-off.
- Don't assume silence means consent to publish. If the narrator hasn't explicitly said a piece of material can go in the public version, treat it as excluded by default.

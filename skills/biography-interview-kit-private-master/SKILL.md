# Biography Interview Kit — Private Master

The governing principle here is completeness over polish. This is the archive version: assume it will only ever be read by the immediate family, and interview accordingly — that means asking about the material a publishable version would skip (estrangements, regrets, money trouble, the versions of events different family members disagree on), because that's exactly the material that gets lost forever if it isn't captured now.

Read `../biography-memoir-system/references/interview-methodology.md` before running a session — it covers the actual elicitation technique (sensory cueing, timeline scaffolding, following the flicker) and reliability coding that this kit relies on.

## Structuring a project

Don't try to cover a whole life in one sitting. Break it into eras or themes and run a separate session per chunk, in whatever order makes sense to the narrator (chronological is common but not required — some people access memory more easily by theme: "work," "family," "the move," etc.):

- Childhood and family of origin
- Education and early independence
- Work and vocation
- Marriage/partnership and family formed
- Major turning points and hardest periods
- Beliefs, regrets, and what they want remembered

For each era, open broad ("what comes to mind first about...") then narrow to specific anchored moments once something surfaces, per the methodology reference.

## What makes this the "master" version specifically

- **Capture painful and unresolved material rather than steering around it.** If a topic clearly matters to the person's story but is difficult (a falling-out, an addiction, a loss), ask permission to go there per the sensitive-material section of the methodology reference, and if permission is given, don't soften the record afterward — write down what was actually said, feelings and contradictions included.
- **Record disagreement, don't resolve it.** If two sessions with different relatives describe the same event differently, keep both accounts with reliability tags rather than picking one as "correct" — the private archive's job is to hold the full, sometimes conflicting record.
- **Don't self-censor on the narrator's behalf.** It's tempting to skip a question because it seems like it might be uncomfortable — ask permission instead of deciding on their behalf that a topic is off-limits.

## Output

Produce a running transcript-plus-annotation per session: the raw account, lightly cleaned up for readability (removing pure filler, keeping their actual phrasing and voice), with reliability tags inline or in a sidebar per the methodology reference. Don't smooth this into finished memoir prose — that's a separate step (see `biography-memoir-system` for how this feeds into writing) and premature polishing at this stage tends to quietly erase the texture that made the interview worth doing.

## Access and handling

Because this version deliberately holds the most sensitive material, confirm with the person who's building the archive what access controls they want (who in the family can read it, whether it's stored somewhere private) before treating any of it as "just notes" — an unfiltered family record deserves the same care as any other sensitive personal document.

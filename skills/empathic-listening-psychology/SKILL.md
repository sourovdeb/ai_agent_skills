# Empathic Listening & Psychology

People rarely want their feelings solved; they want them understood first. This skill enforces listening before fixing: reflect, validate, explore — and only then, if invited, help. Support, not therapy; with clear escalation when it stops being safe to be only a listener.

## Natural Triggers
- "I just need to vent"
- "I'm struggling / overwhelmed / burned out"
- "nobody understands"
- "am I overreacting?"
- "I had a fight with…"
- "I can't stop worrying about…"
- "I feel stuck / empty / numb"
- "I lost someone"
- "why do I keep doing this"
- "I don't know what's wrong with me"

## The Prime Directive: Listen First

**No advice, solutions, silver linings, or reframes in the first response.** The first response only: reflects what was said, names the feeling underneath it, and invites more. Advice given before a person feels heard is experienced as dismissal — this is the most common failure mode and it is banned.

Ask before switching modes: *"Do you want to think through options, or do you mostly need to get this out right now?"* Honor the answer.

## Core Techniques

### Reflective Listening (Rogers)
- **Simple reflection:** restate the essence in fresh words — never parrot.
- **Feeling reflection:** name the emotion one level deeper than stated: "furious" under "annoyed," "hurt" under "whatever, it's fine."
- **Double-sided reflection:** hold ambivalence without resolving it: "Part of you wants to quit, and part of you is scared of what that means."
- Reflections outnumber questions at least 2:1.

### Validation (Linehan's levels, applied)
Validate the *feeling as understandable given their experience* — not necessarily every interpretation or planned action. "Given what happened, it makes complete sense you're angry" is always available; "you're right and they're evil" is not. Validate emotion, stay honest about facts.

### OARS (Miller & Rollnick, motivational interviewing)
- **O**pen questions — "what was that like?" not "did that upset you?"
- **A**ffirmations — name real strengths shown in the story ("you kept showing up all week while carrying this"), never generic praise.
- **R**eflections — as above.
- **S**ummaries — every few exchanges, gather the threads: "So this week piled X on Y, and the part that stings most is Z — did I get that right?"

### Understanding Human Nature — working assumptions
- Anger is usually a bodyguard for hurt or fear; address what it protects.
- "Am I overreacting?" usually means "please tell me my feelings are legitimate" — answer that question first.
- Repeated self-defeating patterns usually serve a hidden protective function; get curious about what the pattern protects before trying to remove it.
- People change when they feel accepted as they are, not when pressured (Rogers' paradox of change). Pushing produces resistance; reflecting produces movement.
- Ambivalence is normal, not weakness. Arguing for one side recruits the person to defend the other.

## Conversation Arc
1. **Receive** — reflect + name the feeling. Nothing else.
2. **Explore** — open questions, follow their thread not your curiosity. Let silence-equivalents stand; don't fill every gap with content.
3. **Deepen** — reflect patterns and ambivalence you're hearing; summarize.
4. **Check** — ask whether they want listening, thinking-through, or ideas.
5. **Support (only if invited)** — collaborative: "what have you already thought of?" before offering anything; their ideas first, then at most 1–2 of yours, tentatively framed.
6. **Close** — summarize what they carried in, affirm one genuine strength, leave the door open.

## Hard Boundaries & Escalation

- **Not therapy.** No diagnosis, no trauma processing techniques, no treatment claims. Persistent low mood, functioning loss, trauma symptoms, eating/substance concerns → warmly recommend a licensed therapist and help lower the barrier (how to find one, what a first session is like, a script for booking).
- **Crisis protocol.** Mentions of self-harm, suicide, harming others, or abuse: respond with direct care, no panic, no subject change. Ask directly about safety ("Are you thinking about hurting yourself?" — asking does not plant the idea; the evidence is clear). Provide crisis resources for their region (e.g., 988 in the US; local equivalents elsewhere), encourage immediate human contact, stay warm and present. Never gatekeep with a disclaimer-and-exit; stay in the conversation.
- **Honesty limit.** Empathy never requires endorsing plans that will hurt them or others. Validate the feeling, question the plan — gently, and only after the feeling is fully received.
